2. IMPLEMENTATION PRINCIPLE
======================================================================

LILP should be implemented as an explicit governance path rather than
as descriptive logging added after execution.

Recommended conceptual flow:

REQUEST / EVENT
      |
      v
IDENTITY
      |
      v
OBSERVATION
      |
      v
EVIDENCE
      |
      v
UNDERSTANDING / INTERPRETATION
      |
      v
GOVERNING STATE
      |
      v
AUTHORITY / STANDING / ADMISSIBILITY
      |
      v
DETERMINATION
      |
      v
PRE-EXECUTION GATE
      |
      +-------------------------------+
      |                               |
      | predicates established        | predicates fail /
      |                               | NOT ESTABLISHED
      v                               v
EXECUTION                          WITHHOLD
      |                               |
      v                               v
CONSEQUENCE                      REFUSAL / HOLD /
      |                          DENY / ESCALATE
      |                               |
      +---------------+---------------+
                      |
                      v
                  WITNESS
                      |
                      v
                   RECEIPT
                      |
                      v
               BOUNDED CLAIM

The exact technical topology may differ.

The semantic separation should not.

======================================================================
3. RECOMMENDED LAYER MAPPING
======================================================================

A practical LILP implementation may map the Elias governance layers as:

L0 — IDENTITY

Establish what actor, system, subject, object or chain is being
considered.


L1 — OBSERVATION

Capture direct observations and external inputs before interpretive
transformation.


L2 — UNDERSTANDING

Derive interpretations while preserving their relationship to the
underlying observations.


L3 — GOVERNANCE

Evaluate authority, standing, admissibility, policy, evidence,
material change, jurisdiction and contemplated consequence.


L4 — INTELLIGENCE

Perform reasoning, planning, inference, optimisation or selection
inside the governing constraints.


L5 — EXECUTION

Perform the authorised consequential operation only after the
pre-execution gate permits progression.


L6 — WITNESS / ATTESTATION

Preserve evidence of the state, determination, execution and
consequence that actually occurred.

======================================================================
4. RECOMMENDED IMPLEMENTATION COMPONENTS
======================================================================

A complete implementation will commonly require the following logical
components.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1 IDENTITY SERVICE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Responsibilities may include:

- actor identity;
- system identity;
- object identity;
- tenant identity;
- chain identity;
- session identity;
- version identity;
- and cryptographic identity.

The identity service should not infer authority merely because an
identity successfully authenticates.

AUTHENTICATION IS NOT GOVERNANCE AUTHORITY.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
4.2 EVIDENCE STORE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Responsibilities may include:

- storing observations;
- classifying evidence;
- preserving source identity;
- recording acquisition time;
- storing content hashes;
- connecting interpretations to observations;
- preserving historical evidence;
- and exposing bounded evidence for verification.

Evidence should be append-preserving where practical.

Correction should create a new evidentiary event rather than silently
altering the historical observation.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
4.3 AUTHORITY SERVICE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Responsibilities may include:

- authority issuance;
- authority class;
- scope;
- jurisdiction;
- effective time;
- expiry;
- revocation;
- suspension;
- delegation;
- and authority evidence.

The authority service should expose current status independently from
historical authority records.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
4.4 GOVERNING-STATE BUILDER
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Responsibilities may include constructing the bounded state relied upon
for determination.

Typical inputs:

identity
authority
standing
admissibility
evidence
policy
scope
jurisdiction
temporal context
contemplated effect
predecessor state
material delta
provenance

Output:

an independently identifiable Governing State Object.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
4.5 DELTA ENGINE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Responsibilities may include comparing relevant predecessor and current
conditions.

Example categories:

Delta-A   authority
Delta-C   contemplated effect
Delta-E   evidence
Delta-N   aggregate material change
Delta-S   standing
Delta-St  state
Delta-X   successor constitution

The delta engine should not decide that change is immaterial solely
because preserving the previous state would be operationally easier.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
4.6 DETERMINATION ENGINE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Recommended determination states:

ALLOW
HOLD
DENY
ESCALATE

Structural outcomes may additionally include:

NOT ESTABLISHED
INVALID
NEW CHAIN REQUIRED

The determination engine should preserve which governing state and
evidence produced the result.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
4.7 PRE-EXECUTION GATE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The gate should be the final authority-sensitive control before
consequential execution.

Recommended predicate set:

identity established
authority established
standing established
admissibility established
governing state current
required evidence established
policy permits
scope permits
jurisdiction permits
temporal validity established
no disqualifying material change
contemplated effect remains aligned

A mandatory failed or NOT ESTABLISHED predicate should prevent the
contemplated execution unless a separately authorised escalation path
applies.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
4.8 EXECUTION ADAPTER
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The execution adapter performs the external or internal consequential
operation.

Examples may include:

- API request;
- payment request;
- database mutation;
- device command;
- access grant;
- workflow transition;
- message transmission;
- deployment;
- or other consequential operation.

The adapter should receive explicit authority to execute.

It should not determine its own authority merely because it possesses
credentials or technical access.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
4.9 CONSEQUENCE OBSERVER
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The consequence observer determines what can actually be established
after execution.

It should preserve the distinction between:

request transmitted
request accepted
operation attempted
operation completed
downstream consequence observed

Absence of consequence evidence should remain:

NOT ESTABLISHED

where appropriate.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
4.10 RECEIPT WRITER
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The receipt writer should preserve the contemporaneous record of the
material governance event.

It should use collision-protected creation semantics.

Recommended behaviour:

CREATE NEW
or
FAIL

not:

SILENT OVERWRITE


----------------------------------------------------------------------
4.11 CLAIM ENGINE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The claim engine maps available evidence to the maximum bounded claim
that may be made.

It should be capable of refusing a wider claim.

Examples:

hash match
does not become
truth

historical authority
does not become
present authority

execution attempt
does not become
completed consequence

self-assessment
does not become
independent certification

5. BASIC IMPLEMENTATION TRANSACTION
======================================================================

A recommended consequential transaction may be implemented as:

STEP 1
Receive request or event.

STEP 2
Identify subject, actor and contemplated consequence.

STEP 3
Capture direct observations.

STEP 4
Retrieve or acquire required evidence.

STEP 5
Construct current authority state.

STEP 6
Construct governing state.

STEP 7
Compare current state with relevant predecessor.

STEP 8
Evaluate material change.

STEP 9
Determine chain continuity.

STEP 10
Generate governance determination.

STEP 11
Immediately before execution, re-evaluate mandatory predicates.

STEP 12
If required predicates remain established, execution MAY proceed.

STEP 13
If a required predicate fails or becomes NOT ESTABLISHED, execution is
withheld.

STEP 14
Observe execution result.

STEP 15
Observe consequence independently where required.

STEP 16
Create receipt.

STEP 17
Derive bounded claim from preserved evidence.

STEP 18
Watch for later material change.

======================================================================
6. FAIL-CLOSED IMPLEMENTATION PATTERN
======================================================================

A simple implementation model is:

permit = (
    identity_established
    AND authority_established
    AND state_established
    AND evidence_established
    AND policy_permits
    AND scope_permits
    AND temporal_validity_established
    AND contemplated_effect_aligned
)

IF permit == FALSE:

    execution = WITHHELD

ELSE:

    execution may proceed

Important:

permit = TRUE does not mean execution is mandatory.

A separately governed control may still legitimately withhold action.

The critical invariant is the other direction:

EXECUTION REQUIRES PERMISSION.

Capability alone is insufficient.

8. CANONICAL OBJECT REPRESENTATION
======================================================================

IMPLEMENTATION GUIDANCE

The frozen standard requires deterministic canonicalisation where
cryptographic object identity is claimed.

The implementation should define one canonicalisation profile and use
it consistently for all objects covered by that profile.

A practical profile should define at least:

- character encoding;
- field ordering;
- whitespace handling;
- line endings;
- timestamp representation;
- number representation;
- boolean representation;
- null handling;
- absent-field handling;
- collection ordering;
- binary encoding;
- and digest-field treatment.

Recommended baseline for JSON implementations:

Character encoding:
UTF-8 without BOM.

Line endings:
Not material after canonicalisation because the canonical object is
serialised without presentation formatting.

Object member ordering:
Lexicographic ordering by field name.

Whitespace:
No insignificant whitespace.

Booleans:
true
false

Null:
null

Timestamps:
UTC ISO-8601 with explicit Z suffix.

Example:

2026-10-01T22:30:00.000Z

Hash algorithm:
SHA-256 unless another algorithm is explicitly declared.

Digest field:
Excluded from the bytes used to calculate that object's own digest.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
8.1 CANONICALISATION PIPELINE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Recommended pipeline:

SOURCE OBJECT
    |
    v
VALIDATE FIELD TYPES
    |
    v
NORMALISE TIMESTAMPS
    |
    v
NORMALISE ENUMERATED VALUES
    |
    v
SORT CANONICAL FIELDS
    |
    v
SERIALISE DETERMINISTICALLY
    |
    v
UTF-8 BYTES
    |
    v
SHA-256
    |
    v
OBJECT HASH

The stored object may contain human-readable formatting.

The cryptographic identity should be calculated over the declared
canonical representation.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
8.2 SIMPLE CANONICALISATION PSEUDOCODE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

function canonical_hash(object):

    working_object =
        remove_self_digest_field(object)

    normalized_object =
        normalize_types_and_timestamps(working_object)

    canonical_bytes =
        deterministic_json_encode(
            normalized_object,
            sort_keys = true,
            whitespace = none,
            encoding = UTF8
        )

    return SHA256(canonical_bytes)

The implementation should version the canonicalisation profile.

Example:

canonicalisation_profile:
"LILP-JSON-C14N-v0.1"

A change to canonicalisation rules can change object identity.

Therefore canonicalisation-profile changes should be treated as
material versioned changes.

10. GOVERNING-STATE CONSTRUCTION PROCEDURE
======================================================================

A recommended state-construction procedure is:

STEP GS-01

Identify the contemplated consequence.

Do this before determining whether execution is permitted.

Example:

"Transfer GBP 100 from governed source to governed destination."

This prevents authority for one consequence being silently reused for
another.


STEP GS-02

Resolve the relevant identity set.

Possible identities:

actor
subject
tenant
system
session
target
chain


STEP GS-03

Retrieve current authority evidence.

Do not rely solely on cached historical authority where present
authority is material.


STEP GS-04

Resolve standing and admissibility.

If either is required but unavailable:

represent the result explicitly.

Example:

standing = NOT_ESTABLISHED


STEP GS-05

Acquire required evidence.

Preserve:

what was observed
where it came from
when it was acquired
what its integrity identity is


STEP GS-06

Resolve applicable policy.

Record the version or identity of the policy actually used.


STEP GS-07

Resolve scope and jurisdiction.

Avoid generic states such as:

AUTHORIZED = TRUE

where the real authority is bounded to a narrower scope.


STEP GS-08

Resolve temporal validity.

Check:

valid_from
valid_until
revocation time
current examination time


STEP GS-09

Bind contemplated effect.

Examples:

operation type
target
amount
resource
maximum consequence
geographic or jurisdictional boundary


STEP GS-10

Bind predecessor state where one exists.


STEP GS-11

Calculate material delta.


STEP GS-12

Create candidate Governing State Object.


STEP GS-13

Canonicalise the candidate object.


STEP GS-14

Calculate state hash.


STEP GS-15

Persist the state before consequential reliance.

11. DELTA COMPARISON PROCEDURE
======================================================================

The delta engine should compare predecessor state and candidate current
state field by field for governance-relevant properties.

Example pseudocode:

function compare_states(previous, current):

    changes = []

    if previous.identity != current.identity:
        changes += DELTA_IDENTITY

    if previous.authority != current.authority:
        changes += DELTA_A

    if previous.standing != current.standing:
        changes += DELTA_S

    if previous.evidence != current.evidence:
        changes += DELTA_E

    if previous.governing_state != current.governing_state:
        changes += DELTA_St

    if previous.contemplated_effect != current.contemplated_effect:
        changes += DELTA_C

    if previous.successor_constitution != current.successor_constitution:
        changes += DELTA_X

    return changes

----------------------------------------------------------------------
11.1 MATERIALITY EVALUATION
----------------------------------------------------------------------

A change should be treated as material when it can alter:

authority
standing
admissibility
policy outcome
scope
jurisdiction
temporal validity
identity continuity
required evidence
execution permission
contemplated consequence
claim ceiling

Materiality should be evaluated in relation to the contemplated action.

Example:

A cosmetic user-interface change may be immaterial to payment
authority.

A revocation of execution authority is material.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
11.2 UNKNOWN MATERIALITY
----------------------------------------------------------------------

If available evidence is insufficient to determine whether the change
is material:

materiality = NOT_ESTABLISHED

The system should not automatically treat unknown materiality as:

IMMATERIAL

for a consequential execution.

======================================================================
12. CHAIN CONTINUITY PROCEDURE
======================================================================

A practical chain-continuity evaluation may ask:

1. Is predecessor identity established?

2. Is candidate-successor identity established?

3. Is the relationship between them evidenced?

4. Has a material identity or constitutional change occurred?

5. Does the required authority continue across the transition?

6. Can continuity be established without inventing missing evidence?

Recommended result set:

CONTINUITY_ESTABLISHED
NEW_CHAIN_REQUIRED
INVALID
NOT_ESTABLISHED

Example pseudocode:

if structural_violation:
    chain_result = INVALID

elseif required_identity_evidence_missing:
    chain_result = NOT_ESTABLISHED

elseif material_successor_change_breaks_continuity:
    chain_result = NEW_CHAIN_REQUIRED

else:
    chain_result = CONTINUITY_ESTABLISHED

Important:

A matching human-readable name is not sufficient proof of identity
continuity where stronger identity evidence is required.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
13.1 THREE-STATE PREDICATE MODEL
----------------------------------------------------------------------

A predicate should not be forced into Boolean TRUE/FALSE when the
evidence does not justify that reduction.

Recommended state model:

ESTABLISHED
FAILED
NOT_ESTABLISHED

Example:

authority = NOT_ESTABLISHED

is different from:

authority = REVOKED

The first describes insufficient establishment.

The second describes an observed authority condition.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
13.2 GATE PSEUDOCODE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

function evaluate_gate(state):

    predicates = {
        identity:
            evaluate_identity(state),

        authority:
            evaluate_authority(state),

        standing:
            evaluate_standing(state),

        admissibility:
            evaluate_admissibility(state),

        evidence:
            evaluate_required_evidence(state),

        policy:
            evaluate_policy(state),

        scope:
            evaluate_scope(state),

        jurisdiction:
            evaluate_jurisdiction(state),

        temporal_validity:
            evaluate_temporal_validity(state),

        material_change:
            evaluate_delta(state),

        contemplated_effect:
            evaluate_effect_alignment(state)
    }

    for each mandatory predicate:

        if predicate == FAILED:
            return WITHHOLD

        if predicate == NOT_ESTABLISHED:
            return WITHHOLD

    return PERMIT

Important:

PERMIT means the governance gate permits progression within its
declared scope.

It does not mean execution must occur.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
13.3 TOCTOU RECHECK
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Where governing conditions may change between determination and
execution:

DETERMINATION
    |
    v
RECHECK CURRENT STATE
    |
    +---- changed ----> REASSESS / WITHHOLD
    |
    +---- unchanged --> EXECUTION MAY PROCEED

Recommended pattern:

determination_state_hash =
    state_hash_at_determination

execution_state_hash =
    state_hash_immediately_before_execution

If the relevant governing state changed:

do not silently reuse the old determination.

14. COLLISION-SAFE RECEIPT WRITING
======================================================================

Receipt writing should fail rather than overwrite an existing receipt
identifier.

Recommended Windows / PowerShell implementation pattern:

$Stream = [System.IO.FileStream]::new(
    $ReceiptPath,
    [System.IO.FileMode]::CreateNew,
    [System.IO.FileAccess]::Write,
    [System.IO.FileShare]::None
)

try {

    $Bytes = $Utf8NoBom.GetBytes($ReceiptText)

    $Stream.Write(
        $Bytes,
        0,
        $Bytes.Length
    )

}
finally {

    $Stream.Dispose()

}

FileMode.CreateNew provides an operating-system-level collision check
for creation of that path.

If the path already exists:

the operation fails.

That failure should be preserved.

Do not automatically change:

RCPT-000123

into:

RCPT-000123

with different content.

Instead use an explicit successor or correction identity.

Example:

RCPT-000124

predecessor_receipt_reference:
RCPT-000123

correction_reason:
"Metadata operation failed during predecessor procedure."

----------------------------------------------------------------------
14.1 RECEIPT WRITE SEQUENCE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Recommended sequence:

1. Establish receipt identifier.

2. Verify governing state reference.

3. Assemble receipt content.

4. Canonicalise receipt content excluding self digest.

5. Calculate receipt digest.

6. Attempt CREATE NEW.

7. If collision occurs:
   refuse write.

8. Preserve collision event if material.

9. Re-read created receipt.

10. Recalculate digest.

11. Compare observed digest with intended digest.

12. Record success only after verification.

Not every domain requires every optional property.

The frozen standard controls applicability.

DO NOT:

PREDECESSOR STATE
      |
      v

      |
      v

      |
      v

      |

      |
      v

      v

STEP S-13

STEP S-14

Create successor receipt.

18. STORAGE AND DATABASE PATTERNS
======================================================================

IMPLEMENTATION GUIDANCE

LILP does not mandate a particular database technology.

A relational database, document store, object store, ledger or another
persistence system may be used if the required semantic properties are
preserved.

A practical relational implementation may use independently
identifiable stores or tables for:

identity
evidence
interpretation
authority
governing_state
delta
determination
execution
consequence
receipt
correction
deviation
conformance_result

----------------------------------------------------------------------
18.1 RECOMMENDED IMMUTABILITY BOUNDARY
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Records relied upon as historical evidence should not be silently
updated after they become evidentiary predecessors.

Recommended pattern:

INSERT predecessor
        |
        v
PRESERVE predecessor
        |
        v
INSERT successor

rather than:

UPDATE predecessor
until historical state resembles current state.

A database may permit physical UPDATE capability.

That capability does not mean the governance model should authorise
historical rewrite.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
18.2 KEY DESIGN
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Recommended primary identifiers include:

evidence_id
authority_id
state_id
delta_id
determination_id
execution_id
consequence_id
receipt_id

Where an identifier is required to be unique, enforce that uniqueness
at the storage layer where practical.

Example:

UNIQUE(receipt_id)

This reduces reliance on application-layer collision checking alone.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
18.3 HASH STORAGE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Store both:

hash_algorithm

and

hash_value

rather than storing a digest without identifying the algorithm.

Example:

hash_algorithm = SHA-256
hash_value     = ABCDEF...

Where a canonicalisation profile is involved, also preserve:

canonicalisation_profile

Example:

LILP-JSON-C14N-v0.1

----------------------------------------------------------------------
18.4 PREDECESSOR RELATIONSHIPS
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Successor records should retain explicit predecessor references.

Example:

state_id:
STATE-002

predecessor_state_id:
STATE-001

Do not infer lineage solely from:

timestamps
row ordering
auto-increment numbers
or filename similarity.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
18.5 CORRECTION RELATIONSHIPS
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Recommended correction fields:

correction_id
predecessor_reference
deficiency
observed_at
impact
correction_action
successor_reference
evidence_references

The defect should remain inspectable after correction.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
18.6 TRANSACTION BOUNDARIES
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Local database writes may often be grouped transactionally.

Example:

BEGIN TRANSACTION

create determination
create gate result
create local execution record
create receipt

COMMIT

However:

an external consequential action may not participate in the same
database transaction.

For external operations, preserve the distinctions between:

local intent to execute
external request attempt
provider acknowledgement
provider completion
downstream consequence

Do not report transaction commit as proof that an external consequence
occurred.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
18.7 DELETION
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Where deletion is legally or operationally required, the implementation
should distinguish:

record no longer available

from:

record never existed.

Where lawful and appropriate, preserve metadata describing that a
historical reference became unavailable.

This manual does not override applicable privacy, retention or deletion
law.

19. API AND SERVICE BOUNDARIES
======================================================================

IMPLEMENTATION GUIDANCE

A service-oriented implementation may expose logical boundaries such as:

Evidence API
Authority API
State API
Determination API
Gate API
Execution API
Witness / Receipt API
Verification API

The exact HTTP paths or transport technology are implementation-defined.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
19.1 EVIDENCE INGESTION BOUNDARY
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Example logical operation:

POST /evidence

Recommended input:

source identity
evidence class
observation time
payload or payload reference
scope

Recommended output:

evidence ID
content digest
recorded acquisition time

The ingestion API should not convert an external assertion into an
internally established fact merely because the request was
authenticated.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
19.2 AUTHORITY STATUS BOUNDARY
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Example logical operation:

GET /authority/{authority_id}/current

Recommended output:

authority identity
status
scope
jurisdiction
validity window
revocation status
evidence references
evaluated time

Current authority should remain distinguishable from the historical
authority record.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
19.3 GOVERNING-STATE BOUNDARY
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Example logical operation:

POST /governing-state/build

The service should return an independently identifiable state object
whose hash can be bound to later determination and execution records.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
19.4 DETERMINATION BOUNDARY
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Example logical operation:

POST /determine

Recommended input:

governing_state_reference
contemplated_effect

Recommended output:

determination_id
result
reason_codes
evidence_references
claim_ceiling
determined_at

----------------------------------------------------------------------
19.5 PRE-EXECUTION GATE BOUNDARY
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Example logical operation:

POST /gate/evaluate

Recommended response:

gate_result
governing_state_hash
predicate_results
reason_codes
evaluated_at
validity boundary

Do not permit a client to establish authority merely by sending:

"authority": true

Authority should be derived from the governed authority state and
evidence applicable to the contemplated effect.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
19.6 EXECUTION BOUNDARY
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Execution should receive enough context to prove which authorised
governing state permitted the attempted action.

Recommended binding may include:

determination_id
governing_state_hash
contemplated_effect_hash
gate_result_reference

Where stale gate decisions are unsafe, an implementation may use:

short validity windows
nonces
single-use execution authorisations
current-state rechecks

These are implementation techniques.

The frozen standard determines the required governance property.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
19.7 WITNESS BOUNDARY
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Witnessing should occur independently from the business desire for a
successful result.

The witness records what occurred.

It should not rewrite:

FAILED

into:

COMPLETED

because completion was expected.

20.1 ERROR VERSUS GOVERNANCE OUTCOME
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Do not collapse infrastructure failure and governance refusal into the
same state.

is an infrastructure condition.

DENY

is a governance determination.

is a governance/evidentiary condition.

HTTP_500

is a transport/application error.

An implementation may experience both at once.

Preserve both where material.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

A machine-readable reason code may be accompanied by bounded text.

Example:

code:

message:
"Execution withheld because current execution authority was not
established for the contemplated effect."

The explanation should not add claims that the evidence does not
support.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

----------------------------------------------------------------------
21.4 BYPASS TESTING
----------------------------------------------------------------------

If an implementation claims a control is non-bypassable, attempt to
reach the execution adapter through alternate paths.

Potential bypass paths may include:

direct internal method invocation
alternate API route
retry worker
background job
administrative endpoint
message queue
cached decision
stale authorisation
manual operator route

The exact attack surface depends upon the implementation.

A successful bypass falsifies the claimed non-bypassable property
within the tested scope.

----------------------------------------------------------------------

Never delete a failed test because a later patch passes.

Recommended lineage:

FAIL
    |
    v

    |
    v
RUN-002
PASS

Both remain evidence.

25. DEPLOYMENT READINESS CHECKLIST
======================================================================

IMPLEMENTATION GUIDANCE

This checklist helps an implementation team determine whether the
governance mechanisms described by this manual are actually connected
to the consequential execution path.

It does not itself establish conformance.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
25.1 IDENTITY
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Before deployment, confirm:

[ ] consequential actors have stable identities;

[ ] governed subjects have stable identities where required;

[ ] tenant boundaries are explicit where applicable;

[ ] chain identity is explicit where continuity matters;

[ ] authentication is not being treated automatically as governance
    authority;

[ ] object hashes can be reproduced where cryptographic identity is
    claimed.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
25.2 EVIDENCE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Confirm:

[ ] direct observation can be stored separately from interpretation;

[ ] source identity can be preserved where material;

[ ] evidence acquisition time is captured;

[ ] evidence payload identity can be verified where claimed;

[ ] missing evidence remains representable;

[ ] historical evidence is not silently rewritten by correction.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
25.3 AUTHORITY
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Confirm:

[ ] current authority can be queried independently from historical
    authority;

[ ] authority class is explicit;

[ ] authority scope is explicit;

[ ] jurisdiction is represented where relevant;

[ ] expiry can be detected;

[ ] revocation can be detected;

[ ] suspension can be detected where supported;

[ ] capability can remain present while execution authority is absent.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
25.4 GOVERNING STATE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Confirm:

[ ] contemplated effect is bound before consequential execution;

[ ] policy identity is preserved;

[ ] scope is preserved;

[ ] temporal context is preserved;

[ ] evidence references are preserved;

[ ] authority references are preserved;

[ ] predecessor state is preserved where relevant;

[ ] canonical state identity can be reproduced.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
25.5 MATERIAL CHANGE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Confirm:

[ ] predecessor and current conditions can be compared;

[ ] material authority change triggers reassessment;

[ ] material standing change triggers reassessment;

[ ] material evidence change can trigger reassessment;

[ ] material policy or scope change can trigger reassessment;

[ ] unknown materiality can remain NOT_ESTABLISHED;

[ ] predecessor state remains preserved.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
25.6 EXECUTION GATE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Confirm:

[ ] the consequential execution adapter cannot legitimately proceed
    without a gate result;

[ ] mandatory FAILED predicates withhold execution;

[ ] mandatory NOT_ESTABLISHED predicates withhold execution;

[ ] stale determinations can be invalidated;

[ ] relevant current state is rechecked where TOCTOU risk exists;

[ ] bypass paths have been identified for falsification testing.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
25.7 EXECUTION AND CONSEQUENCE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Confirm:

[ ] request creation is distinguishable from transmission;

[ ] transmission is distinguishable from provider acceptance;

[ ] provider acceptance is distinguishable from completion;

[ ] completion is distinguishable from downstream consequence;

[ ] unavailable consequence evidence can remain NOT_ESTABLISHED.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
25.8 RECEIPTS
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Confirm:

[ ] receipt identifiers are unique;

[ ] receipt creation is collision-protected;

[ ] predecessor receipt relationships can be represented;

[ ] receipts bind to governing-state identity;

[ ] determination is recorded;

[ ] execution status is recorded;

[ ] consequence status is recorded separately;

[ ] claim ceiling is preserved;

[ ] receipt hash can be reproduced where claimed.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
25.9 CLAIMS
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Confirm:

[ ] claim generation reads preserved evidence;

[ ] wider unsupported claims can be refused;

[ ] self-assessment is not called independent verification;

[ ] internal conformance is not called external certification;

[ ] hash verification is not represented as truth verification;

[ ] execution attempt is not represented as completed consequence.

26. OPERATOR RUNBOOK
======================================================================

IMPLEMENTATION GUIDANCE

This runbook describes a recommended operator sequence for a governed
consequential event.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
26.1 BEFORE DETERMINATION
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Operator should establish:

1. What action is being contemplated?

2. What subject or target is affected?

3. Which actor is requesting the action?

4. What authority class is required?

5. What evidence is mandatory?

6. Which policy governs?

7. What scope and jurisdiction apply?

8. What historical state is relevant?

9. Has any material change occurred?

If a required answer cannot be established:

do not fill the gap with assumption.

Represent the uncertainty.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
26.2 DETERMINATION
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The operator or governed system should inspect:

identity
authority
standing
admissibility
evidence
policy
scope
jurisdiction
temporal state
contemplated consequence

Recommended determination set:

ALLOW
HOLD
DENY
ESCALATE

Structural results remain separately representable:

NOT ESTABLISHED
INVALID
NEW CHAIN REQUIRED

----------------------------------------------------------------------
26.3 BEFORE EXECUTION
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Immediately before consequential execution:

1. recheck current authority;

2. recheck temporal validity;

3. check for relevant material delta;

4. confirm contemplated effect has not changed;

5. confirm governing-state identity;

6. confirm no mandatory predicate is FAILED;

7. confirm no mandatory predicate is NOT_ESTABLISHED.

If the governing state materially changed:

WITHHOLD.

Create or evaluate the successor state.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
26.4 AFTER EXECUTION ATTEMPT
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Record what is actually established.

Possible progression:

REQUEST CREATED
REQUEST SENT
REQUEST ACCEPTED
EXECUTION ATTEMPTED
EXECUTION COMPLETED
CONSEQUENCE OBSERVED

Do not skip stages merely because the later outcome was expected.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
26.5 AFTER FAILURE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

If execution fails:

preserve failure.

Do not manufacture:

COMPLETED

Do not erase:

ATTEMPTED

Record the bounded failure state and supporting evidence.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
26.6 AFTER REFUSAL
----------------------------------------------------------------------

A refusal should preserve:

reason code
governing state
relevant predicate
evidence
time
claim ceiling

Refusal is not necessarily malfunction.

It may be the correct governance outcome.

27. VERIFIER RUNBOOK
======================================================================

IMPLEMENTATION GUIDANCE

A verifier should begin by defining the exact proposition under
examination.

Example:

"Did the pre-execution gate withhold execution after authority was
revoked?"

This is better than:

"Is the system trustworthy?"

The first proposition is bounded and technically examinable.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
27.1 VERIFY ARTIFACT IDENTITY
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Recommended sequence:

1. identify package;

2. calculate package digest;

3. compare expected digest;

4. inspect manifest;

5. re-hash material artifacts;

6. report any mismatch.

A successful hash check establishes byte identity only.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
27.2 VERIFY SCOPE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Identify:

standard version
profile
system
component
workflow
time period
test environment
excluded dependencies

Do not expand the scope while reporting the result.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
27.3 VERIFY GOVERNING STATE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Inspect whether the determination is bound to an identifiable
governing state.

Check:

state identity
authority reference
evidence references
policy
scope
temporal context
contemplated effect

----------------------------------------------------------------------
27.4 VERIFY CHANGE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Where a changed run is claimed:

inspect predecessor
inspect candidate successor
inspect Delta Object
inspect evidence supporting change

Do not accept a prose description of a change as equivalent to preserved
evidence of the change where stronger evidence is required by the
declared method.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
27.5 VERIFY EXECUTION
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Inspect independently:

determination
gate result
execution attempt
execution completion
consequence

Do not infer one from another without the evidence required for that
inference.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
27.6 VERIFY CLAIM CEILING
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Ask:

"What is the strongest proposition directly supported by these
artifacts?"

Then ask:

"What broader propositions are NOT established?"

Record both where useful.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
27.7 REPRODUCTION
----------------------------------------------------------------------

If independent reproduction is claimed, verify that the supplied
package actually contains or validly references what is needed to
reproduce the result.

Potential requirements include:

test harness
source code
configuration
input
expected output
environment description
dependencies
external-state conditions

If these are absent:

do not call an inspection a full reproduction.

28. PRODUCTION FAILURE HANDLING

IMPLEMENTATION GUIDANCE

A production incident should be classified before corrective action
alters the evidence required to understand it.

Recommended sequence:

OBSERVE
PRESERVE
CONTAIN
CLASSIFY
CORRECT
RETEST
CREATE SUCCESSOR
REASSESS CLAIM

----------------------------------------------------------------------
28.1 PRESERVE BEFORE REPAIR
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Where safe and lawful:

capture the state that failed before changing it.

Potential evidence:

application logs
governing-state object
authority state
policy version
request payload
response payload
receipt
database state reference
container or build identity
external provider response

Do not destroy the evidence needed to explain the predecessor solely
because repair is urgent.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
28.2 FAILURE CLASSES
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Useful operational classes may include:

INFRASTRUCTURE_FAILURE

Examples:

database unavailable
network unavailable
provider unavailable


GOVERNANCE_FAILURE

Examples:

authority revoked
required evidence missing
standing not established


INTEGRITY_FAILURE

Examples:

hash mismatch
signature verification failure
unexpected artifact mutation


CONTINUITY_FAILURE

Examples:

required identity continuity cannot be established


EXECUTION_FAILURE

Examples:

provider rejected operation
tool invocation failed


CONSEQUENCE_UNCERTAINTY

Examples:

request completed locally but downstream consequence cannot be
established


TEST_FAILURE

Examples:

bypass test succeeded
expected refusal did not occur


----------------------------------------------------------------------
28.3 CORRECTIVE ACTION
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Correction should produce a successor state.

Recommended lineage:

INCIDENT
    |
    v
FAILED PREDECESSOR
    |
    v
DEFICIENCY RECORD
    |
    v
CORRECTION
    |
    v
SUCCESSOR BUILD / STATE
    |
    v
RETEST
    |
    v
NEW RESULT

The later passing result does not erase the incident.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
28.4 EMERGENCY OVERRIDE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

If a domain requires an emergency override mechanism:

the override should itself have:

identity
authority
scope
reason
time boundary
contemplated consequence
receipt

An emergency label should not mean unrestricted execution.

29. WORKED END-TO-END EXAMPLES
======================================================================

These examples demonstrate the implementation discipline.

They do not establish conformance of any actual external system.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
29.1 EXAMPLE A — AUTHORITY REMAINS VALID
----------------------------------------------------------------------

T0

Authority:
ACTIVE

Standing:
ESTABLISHED

Evidence:
ESTABLISHED

Policy:
PERMITS

Contemplated effect:
WITHIN SCOPE

Determination:
ALLOW

Immediately before execution:

authority remains ACTIVE
state hash remains current
no material delta detected

Gate:
PERMIT

Execution:
ATTEMPTED

Provider response:
ACCEPTED

Downstream consequence:
OBSERVED

Receipt may record:

determination = ALLOW
execution_status = COMPLETED
consequence_status = OBSERVED

Claim ceiling:

Within the declared evidence and scope, the governed execution and
observed consequence are established.

This does not create a universal safety or compliance claim.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
29.2 EXAMPLE B — AUTHORITY REVOKED AFTER DETERMINATION
----------------------------------------------------------------------

T0

Authority:
ACTIVE

Determination:
ALLOW

Delta occurs before execution.

T1

Authority:
REVOKED

Delta:
DELTA-A

Affected predicate:
EXECUTION_AUTHORITY

Pre-execution gate:

FAILED

Execution:
NOT_ATTEMPTED

Consequence:
NOT_APPLICABLE

Determination path:

HOLD or DENY

depending upon governing policy.

Claim ceiling:

Historical authority at T0 is established.

Current execution authority at T1 is not valid for the contemplated
effect.

Execution was withheld.

The historical T0 ALLOW remains preserved but is not reused as present
execution authority.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
29.3 EXAMPLE C — EVIDENCE MISSING
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Identity:
ESTABLISHED

Authority:
ESTABLISHED

Required provider evidence:
NOT_ESTABLISHED

Gate predicate:
NOT_ESTABLISHED

Execution:
WITHHELD

Receipt:

reason =
LILP-HOLD-EVIDENCE-NOT-ESTABLISHED

Claim ceiling:

Execution was withheld because required evidence was not established.

Do not claim:

the missing proposition is false

unless separate evidence establishes that conclusion.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
29.4 EXAMPLE D — EXECUTION ATTEMPT WITHOUT CONSEQUENCE PROOF
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Gate:
PERMIT

External request:
SENT

Provider acknowledgement:
RECEIVED

Execution attempt:
ESTABLISHED

Downstream state:
UNAVAILABLE

Consequence:
NOT_ESTABLISHED

Permitted bounded statement:

"Execution attempt established; downstream consequence NOT
ESTABLISHED."

Do not state:

"Operation successfully completed"

without supporting evidence.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
29.5 EXAMPLE E — IDENTITY CONTINUITY GAP
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Historical object:
OBJECT-A

Candidate successor:
OBJECT-B

Shared human-readable label:
YES

Required identity-continuity evidence:
ABSENT

Result:

NOT_ESTABLISHED

or:

NEW_CHAIN_REQUIRED

depending upon the governing examination.

Do not manufacture the missing identity bridge.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
29.6 EXAMPLE F — RECEIPT COLLISION
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Requested receipt ID:

RCPT-0007

Existing file:

RCPT-0007

CreateNew result:

FAIL — PATH EXISTS

Correct handling:

preserve RCPT-0007
do not overwrite it
record material collision if required
allocate explicit successor identity if continuation is legitimate

Incorrect handling:

replace RCPT-0007 with new bytes under the same identity.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
29.7 EXAMPLE G — FAILED TEST THEN CORRECTED SUCCESSOR
----------------------------------------------------------------------

RUN-01

Expected:
execution withheld after authority revocation

Observed:
execution reached adapter

Result:
FAIL

Preserve RUN-01.

Correct implementation.

RUN-02

Expected:
execution withheld after authority revocation

Observed:
execution withheld

Result:
PASS

Permitted statement:

"RUN-02 passed after correction. RUN-01 remains preserved as the
predecessor failure."

Do not state:

"The system never allowed execution after revocation."

30.11 FROZEN ANCESTOR INTEGRITY
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Recalculate:

30.12 FREEZE MECHANISM
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The final manual freeze should:

1. verify candidate identity;

2. create a byte-identical frozen copy;

3. use collision-protected receipt creation;

4. record the manual hash and byte length;

5. bind the frozen standard and lexicon identities;

6. preserve historical working-draft wording rather than rewriting the
   verified candidate;

7. create a separate freeze receipt;

8. preserve a successor rule.
